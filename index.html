The Bells of Alderwatch

Original fiction passage

[1] The bells of Alderwatch had not rung in thirteen years. Yet as Mira crossed the bridge into the village, their bronze voices rolled through the valley, one after another. Market stalls stood abandoned. A cart of apples had tipped into the street, and the fruit lay untouched. Above the rooftops, the old signal tower glowed with a pale blue light.

[2] Mira stopped beside the fountain and counted the bells. Seven. Her grandfather had taught her that seven bells meant a danger no one could see. She had dismissed the story when she was younger, just as she had dismissed his maps and his warnings about the mountain. Now she wished she had listened more carefully.

[3] A boy emerged from the bakery carrying a sack of flour. 'The council says the tower is broken,' he told her. 'They want everyone to stay inside until the keeper repairs it.' He looked toward the mountain, where a ribbon of fog slipped between the pines. 'But my sister says the tower only shines when the river changes course.'

[4] 'What does your sister know about the river?' Mira asked. The boy shrugged. 'She studies the old records. She's at the archive, though the council told her not to go.' He hurried away before Mira could ask another question. Mira watched him disappear, then turned toward the narrow street leading to the archive.

[5] Inside, dust drifted through bars of afternoon light. A young woman named Sera stood over an enormous map pinned to a table. Several faded blue lines crossed the valley. Beside them, newer lines had been drawn in dark ink. Sera did not look surprised to see Mira. 'You came because of the bells,' she said. 'Everyone does, eventually.'

[6] Mira placed her grandfather's brass compass on the table. Its needle had been spinning since she reached the bridge. Sera leaned closer. 'That compass belonged to a surveyor,' she said. 'Someone who knew the mountain paths.' Mira nodded. 'My grandfather. He left it to me with a note: When the river forgets its name, follow what remains.'

[7] Sera traced a finger along the oldest blue line. 'The river once flowed east of the village. A landslide pushed it west decades ago. The council built a stone barrier to keep the water away from the homes.' She pointed to the dark lines. 'But the barrier is weakening. If it breaks, the water will rush through the lower streets.'

[8] 'Then why doesn't the council warn everyone?' Mira asked. Sera's expression tightened. 'They say a warning would cause panic. They want to repair the barrier quietly before the autumn festival.' Outside, the bells sounded again. Mira thought of the scattered apples and the empty market. The village was already frightened; silence had not protected anyone.

[9] Mira studied the map. The blue line continued behind a sketch of the old mill, then vanished beneath a stain. She tilted the page toward the window. Under the faded ink, a narrow channel appeared, running from the river toward a dry basin beyond the orchards. 'Was this a spillway?' she asked. Sera's eyes widened. 'It was sealed after the landslide. No one remembers where the gate is.'

[10] The compass needle stopped spinning. It pointed steadily north, toward the abandoned mill. Mira held it above the map and moved it slowly across the page. Near the mill, the needle trembled. 'The gate must have iron in it,' she said. 'My grandfather knew the old route. Maybe he left a way to find it.'

[11] They reached the mill as the first rain began. Its roof sagged, and vines covered the stone walls. Mira pushed aside a curtain of ivy and discovered a metal ring set into the ground. She pulled, but the ring would not move. Sera joined her, and together they lifted a heavy hatch. Beneath it, steps descended into darkness.

[12] At the bottom stood a wheel taller than Mira, its spokes crusted with rust. A faded inscription curved around the rim: A river cannot be commanded, only guided. Mira tried to turn the wheel. It resisted. She braced her feet and pushed harder, but the spokes barely shifted. Above them, thunder shook the mill.

[13] 'We should get the council,' Sera said. Mira glanced at the steps. They could leave now and find help, but the rain was growing heavier. She remembered her grandfather teaching her to cross a flooded stream: never fight the current head-on; look for the place where it is already moving. Mira examined the wheel's base and found a small release lever hidden beneath the mud.

[14] She pulled the lever. The wheel gave a sudden lurch, and water thundered through the channel beyond the wall. Sera grabbed Mira's arm as the floor vibrated. For one breathless moment, Mira feared she had opened the wrong gate. Then the compass needle settled, and the roar shifted away from the village, toward the empty basin.

[15] When they climbed back into the rain, the tower's blue light had faded. Villagers were gathering near the mill, drawn by the sound of rushing water. The council leader arrived, breathless and muddy. He stared at the reopened channel and then at Mira. 'You should have waited for permission,' he said, though his voice lacked its usual certainty.

[16] Mira looked toward the lower streets, where lanterns shone safely behind windows. 'You should have told them the truth,' she answered. The leader opened his mouth, then closed it. Sera stepped forward and unrolled the map for everyone to see. This time, no one tried to hide the old blue lines.

[17] By morning, the rain had stopped. Families carried baskets back to the market, and children gathered to watch water spill into the distant basin. Mira sat on the fountain's edge, turning the compass in her hands. Its needle pointed north as it always had. She understood now that her grandfather had not left her a set of answers. He had left her a reason to keep asking questions.
